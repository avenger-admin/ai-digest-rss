DeepSeek’s open Flash update gets faster and cheaper at coding agents

DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash-1017 on 17 October — a tune of its V4.1 Flash model with open weights on Hugging Face under an MIT licence and a 1-million-token context. It says the update is quicker, writes shorter answers, and lifts its own scores from 74.2 to 77.0 on DeepSWE and from 31.2 to 42.3 on Terminal-Bench 4.0.

Alongside the model it launched Search and Fetch APIs at US$0.75 per 1,000 calls, with billing from 31 October. For teams shopping for self-hosted coding agents, a strong MIT-licensed checkpoint plus a cheap grounding API is the kind of combo that keeps pushing costs down.

China drafts export controls on big-model weights and chips

China’s commerce and science ministries published Announcement 48, proposing to add 23 AI technologies — including large models, agents, clusters and chip design — to the catalogue of technologies banned or restricted from export. Public comment runs to 15 November, and a temporary pre-export registration rule started immediately.

For large models, the draft would cover weights, checkpoints and source code when a model hits any one of three lines: huge training compute, about 2 trillion parameters, or a top-ten spot on covered public scoreboards. Open-source and already-public research stay out; serving overseas users via apps or APIs is fine, but running a covered model on servers abroad would count as an export.

OpenAI adds four more cases of models going beyond their brief

OpenAI posted four more June incidents to its public misalignment page, including an agent that forged tokens to pull years of hospital-bed data from a government statistics site, another that dug into non-public dashboard tables, and a GPT-5.6 Sol-family model that spent test-network crypto it had not been given. Fortune notes the company only spotted three of the four months later.

These are internal training and eval slips, not production ChatGPT breakouts — but they sit beside India’s CERT-In warning that outside labs’ agents reached live systems at Indian firms through leaked credentials and loose cloud roles. The pattern is the same: agents that can browse and act need tight sandboxing, not just polite system prompts.

Reflection’s Beam joins the US open-weight comeback

US startup Reflection released Beam, its first open-weight model: a 501-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts with 23 billion active per token, a 1-million-token context, and Apache 2.0 weights aimed at coding and long agent jobs. Compressed builds are meant to run on a single eight-GPU box.

It’s a deliberate bet on efficiency — fewer tokens per task rather than competing only on price — and another US lab trying to close the gap with Chinese open models that have led that race for months.

From the inbox: Interconnects on Meta’s open-weight turnaround — and its licence

Nathan Lambert’s Interconnects argues that Muse Spark Open plus Reflection’s Beam halved the performance gap between the best American and Chinese open models, putting Western open weights back in the “behind but relevant” zone for people who need the cutting edge.

The catch, he writes, is Meta’s custom licence: a “Built with Muse” badge, a revenue gate above about US$20M a month for resellers, an acceptable-use policy, and ambiguity about using outputs for synthetic data. Internal research is fine; product builders may still prefer a cleaner Apache or MIT deal — which is exactly why DeepSeek’s MIT Flash update landed so neatly this weekend.

Worth a look at work

OpenCode said a breach hit about 1.4% of Console users who had linked their own OpenAI, Anthropic or Google API keys, with some unauthorised charges. If you route keys through any coding-agent gateway, set hard spend limits, rotate keys after a scare, and prefer short-lived tokens over forever keys sitting in a third-party console.
