Gemini 4 finally has a name, Copilot’s multi-model experiment hits VS Code, and AWS gives OpenAI agents a Bedrock home. Easy skim for Thursday.

Google ships Gemini 4 Argon — but only to trusted cyber defenders (for now)

DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Argon yesterday: a frontier model aimed at long-horizon software engineering, enterprise knowledge work, and cybersecurity defense. It’s already in use inside Google (including large C/C++→Rust migrations) and posts a 1M-token output limit.

Broader access is phased — starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra after more safeguard work. Intro pricing is listed at $2 / $10 per million input/output tokens (rising after the intro period).

GitHub’s HydraFusion leaves the CLI — now in VS Code and the Copilot app

HydraFusion, Copilot’s multi-model research preview, is now in VS Code (1.140+) and the GitHub Copilot app — not just Copilot CLI. It isn’t one model; it picks among Single, Cascade, or Critique workflows to hit a quality bar efficiently.

GitHub also improved transparency and real-time progress updates. Available to Pro, Pro+, Business, and Enterprise (org admins must enable preview features for Business/Enterprise).

Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents (OpenAI-powered) enter preview

AWS and OpenAI jointly launched Bedrock Managed Agents in preview — a customized, AWS-native take on OpenAI’s Agents API. Agents run inside AWS with IAM roles, CloudTrail logging, durable sessions, MCP tools, and optional human approval before consequential actions.

No extra BMA charge during preview beyond the AWS resources you use. Preview regions: US East (N. Virginia), US West (Oregon), and US East (Ohio).

OpenAI says it disrupted a large model-distillation campaign

OpenAI reported shutting down a coordinated effort to extract “protected reasoning” from its models — adversarial distillation meant to help train or copy another model. Operators didn’t hack encryption or databases; they manipulated interactions at scale against the terms of service.

Activity ran from early July, with spikes of ~16k extraction-pattern requests from over 4,000 users, and a related cluster of 15,000+ users disrupted by July 28. Findings were shared via the Frontier Model Forum.

Holo4: open generalist agents that click, code, and call APIs

H Company released Holo4 (27B dense and 35B-A3B MoE) — agentic models trained to use GUIs, code, MCP, and APIs in one stack, aimed at real business workflows rather than a single interface.

Weights are on Hugging Face (including GGUF), with trajectories open-sourced for benchmark replay. On OSWorld 2.0, Holo4 27B scores 61.7% — behind top closed models, but far smaller and cheaper per task.
