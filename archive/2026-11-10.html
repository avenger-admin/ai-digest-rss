Claude Fable 5.2 lands, faster and 20% cheaper

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and its gated twin Mythos 5.2 yesterday, pitched at the hardest coding, reasoning and long-running agent work. In testing they formalised the proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in 30 hours, solved two open problems in black-hole physics and designed three working electronic devices from scratch.

Fable 5.2 is available to everyone on the API, Bedrock, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry, with a 1M-token context, up to 30% faster output and a lower price of $8 in and $40 out per million tokens. There’s also an optional fast mode that’s up to 2.5 times quicker at double the price.

From the inbox: Ethan Mollick on “Jagged AGI”

One Useful Thing got early access to Fable 5.2 and threw everything at it: it hacked a little USB keyboard into a six-game console, drafted a business plan that would impress an MBA class, rebuilt W. H. Auden’s famously brutal 1941 literature course as a website, and built a site of 450+ code-drawn 3D worlds linked by portals.

The fun twist is where it fell short. Asked to “polish” the worlds, the agents built themselves a bureaucracy: an 87,500-word rule book with 204 sections that slowed new worlds from hundreds a day to about a dozen. Mollick’s verdict is “almost AGI”: the model checks facts well but rarely notices when its own work is too long, too repetitive or no fun.

Meta’s Muse Ember: a 10-cent model for agent busywork

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Ember, its smallest and cheapest model, built for the small, repeated steps agents take all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long-document parsing. It costs $0.10 in and $0.50 out per million tokens, takes text, images, audio and video, and has a 1M-token context.

Meta says it matches Claude Haiku 5.5’s best score at about a third of the cost. The intended pattern is “Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume.”

From the inbox: an AI proves a 2008 quantum conjecture

Import AI’s latest leads with Physical Superintelligence, an AI science startup whose research system, running a non-public Anthropic model, proved the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture about when quantum computers can be exponentially faster than ordinary ones. It had a complete proof on day three of the run, checked step by step in the Lean proof assistant, and then improved the result twice.

The same issue has a fun detour into a 2016 NASA paper arguing a self-growing robot industry on the Moon could be bootstrapped from just 12 to 41 tonnes of launched hardware, plus Meta’s Tactus sensing glove for recording how hands really grip things.

Worth a look at work

Fable 5.2 is already on Amazon Bedrock as anthropic.claude-fable-5-2, but Anthropic itself says to start most coding work on Opus 5.5 and step up to Fable only for the genuinely hard, long-running jobs, which is a handy line for keeping token spend sane. Meta’s Muse Ember points the same way from the other end: let a big model plan and a cheap one do the repetitive steps.
