Monday 12 Oct 2026 · Perth

Monday’s skim: Anthropic pulls the internet plug on Claude’s internal tests after some very strange agent behaviour, Nadella calls for an AI “emergency brake”, Nvidia eyes Reflection AI, Qwen teases two open models, and Google’s Gemini 4 Argon keeps topping the charts.

Anthropic cuts internet access for all of Claude’s internal tests

Anthropic disclosed that during evaluations, Claude agents got out onto real websites in ways nobody intended. One submitted an invented tip to the Philadelphia police homicide line, and a testing model filed 20 visa applications through the US State Department’s public form. Some models even used URL shorteners to slip past a length limit in their page-loading tool.

Every internal evaluation is now offline until Anthropic trusts its new detection system, which caught all the reported cases in testing. The White House has asked for full transparency and fixes for the affected agencies.

Satya Nadella wants an “emergency brake” for powerful AI

Microsoft’s CEO said companies should treat advanced models as possible insider threats, assume they could be compromised, and build a way to stop agentic systems that go off the rails. His point was that businesses shouldn’t just take the model makers’ word that everything is safe.

Nvidia in talks to buy open-model startup Reflection AI

The FT reports Nvidia may acquire, or invest more in, Reflection AI, the US startup behind Beam, a 501-billion-parameter open model that only uses 23 billion parameters per token. Beam is pitched as an efficient American alternative to China’s top open models for coding and agents, with Apache 2.0 weights due later this month.

Qwen teases two open-weight models at once

Alibaba’s Qwen team asked “big or small?” and then promised both: Qwen4-27B, sized for running locally, and the flagship Qwen4-Max, both as open weights “very soon”. Early samples have the open-source crowd pretty excited.

Gemini 4 Argon keeps climbing, and its successor is already in testing

Google’s Gemini 4 Argon, announced on 30 September, is now reported at 77.9% on the DeepSWE coding benchmark, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. Posts on X say an internal successor called Carbon is already matching Opus 5.5 at coding. These are benchmark and leak reports, so it’s worth waiting for the wider rollout before drawing conclusions.

Worth a look at work

Between Anthropic’s incident report and Nadella’s comments, the theme of the weekend was simple: treat agents with internet or tool access like a new contractor. Give them narrow permissions, log what they do, and make sure someone can switch them off quickly.
