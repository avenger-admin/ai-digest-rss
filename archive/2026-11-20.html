Anthropic’s first day on the Nasdaq: a big pop, then a calmer close

Anthropic shares opened at $111.50, about 34% above the $83 IPO price, briefly touched around $115, then settled to close at $97.17, up 17%. CNBC puts the fully diluted market cap around $1.8 trillion, making it the seventh most valuable US company, and more than 846 million shares changed hands, beating SpaceX’s opening-day volume.

The wider market wasn’t in a party mood, though. The Nasdaq fell about 2.3% and the S&P 500 notched a sixth straight losing session, so Reuters’ read was that the debut didn’t revive the broader AI trade.

Genie 4 lets you reshape AI worlds while you walk around in them

Google DeepMind has opened Genie 4 to eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie. You generate an interactive world, then type new prompts mid-session to change the weather, drop in a character or redirect the action, and for the first time it comes with real-time native audio, from ambient sound to characters who talk.

There’s also a conversational agent to help you build a world, and a community gallery to explore and remix other people’s. Google is upfront that object permanence and physics can still drift over longer sessions. Pro members can join the waitlist.

OpenAI’s models found up to 14% gains in AMD’s GPU designs

OpenAI says its models rewrote the hardware description code (SystemVerilog RTL) for AMD’s flagship GPU and found up to 14% improvement in power, performance and area against heavily optimised baselines, with each change checked using Synopsys tools. OpenAI calls it roughly a year of progress in under five months, and AMD has already put some changes into a design sent for manufacturing.

AMD also verified single-digit gains on key blocks of its next flagship CPU. Next up is GPT-Synopsys, a specialised model trained to drive chip-design tools the way expert engineers do.

How OpenAI uses Codex agents to keep ChatGPT fast

OpenAI shared its “Performance Factory”: when a performance alert fires, a Codex agent investigates, writes up its findings, and proposes a fix for an engineer to review. After the fix ships, another agent checks whether performance is back to baseline.

In just over three months it tracked more than 1,700 investigations and found the root cause in over 600, about 17 automated investigations a day. It’s a nice, concrete example of agents doing the unglamorous on-call grind with a human still approving the change.

From the inbox: what an AI-powered hack actually looks like

In a free issue of The Pulse, Gergely Orosz unpacks Railway’s postmortem of a recent breach in which the attacker likely used “abliterated” AI models (ones with the safeguards stripped out) to chase customers’ AI provider keys. Within hours of grabbing Railway’s monorepo, the attacker had worked out a path to the keys, and came back probing the same night after being evicted.

The defensive lessons are practical: Railway had stored hashes of customer secrets, so it could send them to AI providers for instant revocation without exposing the keys, and it now runs every build in its own throwaway VM. Gergely also notes a frustrating imbalance: attackers use unrestricted models while mainstream defensive models often refuse security work.

Worth a quick check at work

Microsoft’s agent-framework Python package had two poisoned releases (1.26.1 and 1.26.2) on PyPI on 17 November US time. They were pulled within about an hour, but anything that installed them ran a credential stealer on every Python start, including in CI where it tried to grab GitHub Actions OIDC tokens. We’re mostly TypeScript, so odds are low, but if any Python experiments or pipelines use it, pin 1.26.0 and rotate secrets on anything that pulled the bad versions. GitHub advisory: GHSA-764m-3ppc-5m73.
