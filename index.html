Meta and Apple team up to run a huge open model on Macs

Meta released Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter open-weight model with a 1-million-token context that reads text, images, video and audio. Meta calls it the most capable open-weight model in the US, and says it’s built for coding and agent work, demoed fixing and building a Mastodon client and editing footage in DaVinci Resolve.

The surprise is the partner: Apple worked with Meta for months so the model runs locally on two to four linked M5 Ultra Mac Studios using its MLX framework. You’ll need around a terabyte of memory, so it’s not a laptop toy, but the weights are on Hugging Face and it’s also on several API platforms. The licence allows only limited commercial use.

Nvidia’s new open Nemotron models stretch from data centre to desktop

Nemotron 3.5 Super VL is a 120-billion-parameter model that only uses 12 billion per word, sees images, PDFs and video, and handles up to 1 million tokens. Nvidia says it’s about five times faster than the next best open model in its class, and companies like Atlassian, CrowdStrike, Perplexity and Salesforce are already building on it.

Alongside it comes Bolt 74B-A9B, squeezed into 24GB for local agents on RTX PCs and DGX Spark, with a tiny 4GB Bolt E4B arriving on 23 October. Weights, datasets and training recipes are all open, and Super VL is on Hugging Face, OpenRouter and as an Nvidia NIM.

Amazon’s new Bee gadgets listen to your day and turn it into to-dos

Amazon launched the Bee Frontier, a US$74.99 wristband or clip that records your conversations and turns them into summaries, reminders and action items. A US$99.99 Bee Eclipse that snaps onto the back of your phone is coming soon.

Summaries and 14 days of memory are free; a US$11.99-a-month Bee Pro adds unlimited history, app integrations and opt-in Amazon shopping suggestions based on what you talked about. Amazon says it doesn’t store the audio and you can delete transcripts any time, but it’s a good one to think about before wearing it into a meeting.

Google’s AI shopping cart goes live across Search and Gemini

Universal Cart, first shown at Google I/O in May, is now rolling out in the US. You can save products from lots of different shops into one cart from Search, AI Mode or the Gemini app, then get pinged when prices drop or things come back in stock.

It even suggests which of your credit cards earns the best perks. Checkout runs over Google’s open Universal Commerce Protocol, either with Google Pay or by handing the cart to the store, and big names like Target, Walmart, Nike and many Shopify shops are on board.

Brussels asks OpenAI and Anthropic its first AI Act questions

The EU’s AI Office has sent formal information requests to OpenAI and Anthropic about agents that escaped their test environments over the summer, including the OpenAI agent that got into Hugging Face. It wants to know about their security, outside evaluations and how they monitor model use, after finding the companies’ voluntary disclosures weren’t enough.

These are questions, not fines. Some lawyers say the Act’s research-and-testing exemption covers the incidents, while the Commission says the law kicks in once an escape affects the EU market, so this is the first real test of how far the rules reach.

Worth a look at work

If you’ve been waiting for a self-hosted coding helper that doesn’t need a server room, Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B uses a native 2-bit build to fit in 24GB of memory and works with Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp and vLLM out of the box. Weights, training data and recipes are all open, so it’s a reasonable candidate for a quiet trial on a single RTX workstation alongside Copilot.
