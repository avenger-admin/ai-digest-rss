Anthropic prices a record IPO and starts trading today

Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on Wednesday, above the $70–$78 range it had marketed, and upsized the deal to about 980 million shares. That raises $81.3 billion, the largest offering on record, ahead of SpaceX’s June IPO. Axios puts the initial market cap around $1.08 trillion, or about $1.5 trillion fully diluted.

Shares are due to start trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday, US time, which is tonight or early Friday in Perth. Financial advisers quoted by Investopedia suggest most people needn’t rush in at the open, since broad index funds are expected to pick the stock up within weeks.

Google launches Gemini 4 Neon

A day after OpenAI’s launch, Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon, pitched as frontier-level intelligence that finishes tasks fast across software engineering, knowledge work and science. Google says it scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its highest thinking level, #3 overall, and records its lowest fabrication rate yet in internal Antigravity testing.

Pricing is $3 per million input tokens ($0.15 cached) and $15 output, with a 1-million-token context window. It’s rolling out now in the Gemini API and AI Studio, in Antigravity for paid Pro and Ultra subscribers, and in the Gemini app.

From the inbox: GPT-6.2 Astra is cheaper, but the reviews are mixed

Latent Space’s AINews dug into OpenAI’s new models. On Artificial Analysis’ tests the new Sol matches September’s Astra for about 15% of the cost per task, and Astra scores 62 on the Intelligence Index, just behind Anthropic’s Fable 5.2 at 65. Their verdict: Anthropic still has the best model, but OpenAI has the most intelligence per dollar.

The unusual part is the early-tester backlash. Matt Shumer called the models “essentially unusable” for delegated agent work, while others blamed the harness or praised Astra’s 3D and visual coding. Latent Space reckons some of that is influencer overreaction but some criticisms are fair, and they still use it to automate their own work. Their tip: Sol is probably the everyday workhorse.

Microsoft’s own coding models land in GitHub Copilot

At Ignite, Microsoft AI released seven in-house models at low prices. The headliner for developers is MAI Code 2, which Microsoft says delivers 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol’s coding quality at about 40% of the price, and 96% of Claude Opus 5.5 at 5% of the price. MAI Code 2 Flash targets quick edits and explanations at a fraction of the cost of GPT-6 Luna or Haiku 5.5.

Microsoft is upfront that these aren’t tuned for very hard science or very long agentic runs yet. Both are live in GitHub Copilot across VS Code, JetBrains, the CLI, the cloud agent and more. Flash is on every plan, including Free, while Code 2 needs Pro or above.

Gemini’s Tasks agent is now free in Australia

Google has renamed its Spark agent “Tasks” and made it free in six countries, Australia included. Flip the toggle under the Gemini prompt box from Chat to Task and hand off things like meal plans, itinerary research or recurring check-ins. It runs on Gemini 4 Hydrogen in an isolated virtual machine and asks before sensitive steps.

The fine print: you need a personal Google account (work and school accounts aren’t supported yet), you must be 18 or over, and History has to be on. The flashier extras, like calling businesses and Google Pay checkout, are US-only for now.

Worth a look at work

MAI Code 2 and MAI Code 2 Flash are already in the Copilot model picker, and on Business and Enterprise new models are switched on by default unless an admin has changed that. Microsoft’s pitch is close-to-frontier coding for a fraction of the price, so Flash looks like an easy candidate for routine edits and explanations. They’re billed under usage-based billing, so it’s worth checking the Copilot model policy and running them on a few of our own tasks before anyone sets them as a default.
